# SAM 2 fp16 and V-JEPA bf16 adoption (2026-10-17)

## Decision

Adopt SAM 2 fp16 on MPS and V-JEPA bf16 on MPS. Both precision choices are
recorded in `library/model_registry/registry.json`; SAM segmentation outputs
record their effective precision, and V-JEPA token results carry their
effective precision alongside float32 tokens. CPU fallback remains fp32.

Gemma 4 12B already uses MLX 4-bit (`mlx-community/gemma-4-12b-it-4bit`), so
this change does not alter its runtime. The stack audit found it faster and
smaller than the tested 8-bit and mlx-vlm 0.7.8 variants.

## SAM 2.1 Hiera Small on W02

The source was `IMG_1808.MOV`, sampled at 2 fps (1080x1920). The scheduled
two-target run used the two first-frame boxes from agent A's lane-3 packet as
tracking prompts: P1 as the person seed and O1 as the baseball-cap seed. These
are label-seeded tracking results, not end-to-end detector quality. The task
ran through `heavy_work_lock` with profile
`semantics.analyse:inference` and a declared 10 GB RAM reservation. It finished
after a 324.17 s scheduler wait and 38.06 s execution, at a measured 6.931 GB
process-tree physical-footprint peak. The person track covered 56/56 samples;
the cap track covered 52/56.

The earlier fp32 W02 single-target reference covered 46/56 samples, had mean
mask area ratio 0.0385, ran for 69.2 s and reached 10.143 GB physical
footprint, exceeding the 10 GB ceiling. The fp16 single-target candidate in
the stack audit covered 46/56 samples, had mean mask area ratio 0.03885, ran
for 39.17 s and peaked at 7.066 GiB. Those single-target figures are retained
as context; the two-target run is a separate, label-seeded measurement.

## V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B/384

The exact existing `ema_encoder` checkpoint was measured on 32 frozen
calibration windows, 20 with motion/static labels. Sequential `heavy_work_lock`
runs used the same checkpoint and scorer. The published report's measured
precision comparison is:

| Precision | Warm inference / window | Peak physical footprint | Motion action-ranking AP | P@5 | P@10 | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| fp32 control | 1.500 s | 6.238 GiB | 0.62824 | 0.60 | 0.70 | Reference |
| fp16 | 0.717 s | 4.632 GiB | 0.57855 | 0.60 | 0.50 | Reject: AP -0.04969 |
| bf16 | 0.617 s | 4.639 GiB | 0.62824 | 0.60 | 0.70 | Adopt: quality preserved |

bf16 is 2.43x faster than fp32 and uses 25.6% less sampled physical
footprint. The encoder and input clip use bf16 for MPS inference; output
tokens are converted back to float32 for downstream metrics and caches. The
result includes the effective precision so saved feature provenance can
retain it. CPU inference stays fp32.

## Reproduction and limits

W02 SAM runs used cached weights in offline mode; no checkpoint was
downloaded. The two-target run and short fp32 control wrote scratch outputs
under `/tmp`, not into the Apple 001 project. The blind calibration labels
were read from the lane-3 label packets. The fp32 short comparison is not a
full-window two-target control because the full fp32 run previously crossed
the declared memory ceiling. Quality scores are bbox IoU against agent labels,
not mask IoU or human ground truth. Relation recall is bounded by the absence
of M3 hand landmarks. The V-JEPA calibration and runtime figures above are
from the hardware-stack audit report; no extra V-JEPA model run was needed for
this implementation.
