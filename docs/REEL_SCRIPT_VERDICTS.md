# Are the reel scripts actually good?

The captain's stop condition for the reel work has three parts, and only the
second is about plumbing:

> the scripts for the reels are actually good and sound right; you can create
> them using the pipeline and not any standalone scripts; and there is creative
> reasoning involved in actually creating the reels.

This document answers the first, per reel, in one reader's judgement. It is
measured against the **live approved plan** -
`pipeline_output/review/reel_proposals_v2.json`, nineteen moments, all
`approved` - and the timeline transcript that plan is anchored to. An earlier
review at `data/vep-reel-script-evidence/report.md` graded a **superseded**
fifteen-moment selection with different slugs and different spans; its
definitions are reusable and its per-moment conclusions are not.

Everything below was re-derived on the nineteen.

## What was measured, and what is judgement

Measured: what the build actually plays (`reel_build.reel_ranges` applied to
the plan), the words spoken in the first three seconds of the built reel,
speaker share of body speech, the largest silence inside the body, and the
last words before the closer.

Judgement: whether it reads as a reel somebody watches to the end. That is not
a score and nothing here computes one.

## The three things that were true of the whole set

### No body-span overlaps, and no misattributed line reaches any reel

The superseded set had moments 5 and 6 overlapping, and two lines credited to
both speakers inside built reels. **Neither survives into the nineteen.**

- Zero overlapping body spans across all 190 pairs.
- Five cross-speaker near-identical anchored pairs exist in the episode -
  1201.9s, 1643.7s, 1649.2s, 1656.6s, 1663.4s - and **every one falls in a gap
  between reels**, not inside one. 1201.9s sits between reel 7 (ends 819.1) and
  reel 8 (starts 1343.0); the other four sit between reel 12 (ends 1635.7) and
  reel 13 (starts 1816.0).

So the two defects the earlier review flagged as reaching the video do not
reach this video. That is a real result and it is worth stating as plainly as
the defects.

### Span versus built speech is not a gap in the reel

The earlier review reported up to 37.5s of "difference" between a moment's span
and its speech. That number is **span minus the sum of anchored segment
durations**, which is the silence between sentences. The build plays the whole
span; nothing is lost. On the nineteen that residue runs 3.0s to 21.9s and is
ordinary conversational pause.

What IS real is the review surface: 814 anchored segments carry source data and
61 unanchored ones do not, and a page rendering both shows the reviewer speech
the build ignores - up to 78.2s of it on reel 5. That is a display defect, not
a build defect.

### Seven of nineteen close on the identical sentence

| closer | speaker | reels |
|---|---|---|
| 814.7-819.1 "so if you are watching this definitely check it out on our website also the links in the bio" | Craig | **7** - 6, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19 |
| 321.6-327.2 "we're calling the lucy visibility system we'd love for you to go check it out see how ai sees you" | Craig | **5** - 1, 4, 10, 12, 17 |
| 333.8-341.3 "And that's why we've been building the Lucy visibility system for months..." | Akshita | 2 - 8, 11 |
| 185.3-191.2 "visibility system so if you want to see how you're ranking how ai sees you go to our website" | Craig | 1 - 2 |

Fifteen borrowed closers, four passages. The episode holds 11 CTA-language
passages and at least 7 clean self-contained ones; a clean closer at 1004.7s -
"if you're watching this definitely send us a dm go check it out for yourself" -
was never used at all. **The material was there and the selection collapsed.**

Anyone who watches two of these in a row hears the same ending twice. For those
seven, the honest answer to "do the scripts sound right" is no.

## The nineteen

Length guidance is 45-90s, weighed and never enforced.

### Good as they stand

**8 - why-ai-trusts-a-cited-brand** (64.5s, Craig 20%, max gap 0.6s). The
tightest reel in the set. Craig asks a real question, Akshita answers it whole
with the human analogy carrying the whole way, and it lands on "the moment you
become trustworthy to it, that's when it starts recommending you". Nothing
leans on the episode. Best hook-to-close shape here.

**14 - small-business-beats-the-behemoths** (86.8s, Craig 20%). A reversal
argued end to end and closing on its own contrast, "Great for Google, not good
for AI". Long, and it earns it. One transcription blemish: "needs
human-written... Invoice and authentic storytelling" is "in voice" misheard,
and it will be captioned as written.

**13 - a-score-is-not-a-fix** (53.7s, Craig 19%). A clear turn in the middle -
a score leaves you the work - and it ends on the offer rather than the
complaint. Self-contained.

**16 - how-people-actually-search-now** (72.0s, Craig 16%). Story, insight,
instruction, in that order, and the nail-salon query is concrete. The strongest
piece of storytelling in the set.

**11 - what-hallucinating-actually-means** (77.8s). Craig sets up the
misconception properly and Akshita corrects it to its consequence. Question,
answer, consequence.

**12 - not-a-content-problem** (44.9s, max gap 0.8s). Short, dense, one
correction delivered and finished. Under guidance and better for it.

**19 - can-you-game-ai** (88.1s). Turns the question round rather than
answering it flat. The "Authority and trust" button is good; the trailing
"Yeah." after it is a dangler a trim would remove.

### Weak, and why

**1 - seo-ranks-geo-understands** (50.2s). The content is the cleanest
statement of the episode's premise. **The hook is throat-clearing**: "okay so
i'm hearing just from a lot of different marketing directors". The handoff's
own rule is that a first line must be a question, a claim or a provocation and
never a speaker settling into a sentence, and this is the latter. Moving the
start ~5s into Craig's turn would fix it.

**4 - consistency-beats-size** (47.0s). **Not self-contained.** It opens on the
word "earlier" - "earlier it doesn't even matter if you're a 15 man shop" -
referring to something said outside the reel. A viewer who has not heard the
episode is being asked to remember it. Also a 6.1s silence inside the body, and
three overlapping takes at 444.0/444.4/444.9 where one says AI will recommend
"you over a company that has a lot of sources" and the next says the opposite.

**17 - four-slots-and-nothing-else** (108.1s). **Not self-contained**, for the
same reason: "Let's take that example. You are able to find that nail salon" -
the nail salon is reel 16's example, not this one's. At 108s it is also the
longest, and it ends on "Yeah." before the borrowed closer. The argument
underneath is one of the best in the episode; the packaging is wrong.

**6 - where-ai-is-reading-you** (44.3s, **Craig 10%**). A stumbled hook - "so
what do they so what else do they need to be aware of" - and then forty seconds
of one person. The captain's own definition is a conversation between Craig and
Akshita, not one person talking; 10% is a question and then a monologue.

**10 - the-seven-modules** (71.8s, **Craig 10%**). Same shape, and worse at the
end: the body's last words are "and also checks", cut mid-clause, straight into
the borrowed CTA. It also carries three untimed transcript artifacts
(1474.5, 1474.6, 1504.0) whose text cannot fit their duration.

**2 - seo-that-hurts-your-ai-ranking** (60.4s, **Craig 13%**, **max gap 9.9s**).
Strong story with a real sting, undermined by ten seconds of silence inside it
and by a hook of two words - the first three seconds of the built reel are
"Yeah. So", because the take carrying the setup was cut as redundant.

**7 - website-is-resume** (34.0s, Craig 49%). The image is good and the balance
is the best in the set. But the hook is "Absolutely" - answering a question the
viewer never heard - followed immediately by a stumble, "a hiring manner, hiring
manager". And it says "just like a hiring manager would check both, AI checks
both" twice, at 786.6 and 795.8.

**5 - the-audit-that-was-eye-opening** (78.3s, **max gap 10.3s**). **Tells the
same finding twice**: 559.4-567.2 and 588.3-597.5 are both "we ran the audit,
they ranked top three on Google, they were invisible or misdescribed on AI".
One take was cut; the other survived. Plus ten seconds of silence.

**15 - why-youtube-outranks-other-video** (61.9s). Good hook, clean answer, and
then a **6.4s hole at 2054-2062** with the sentence "Make sure there's a very
strong, concise," left dangling and never finished.

**9 - first-step-is-understanding** (44.6s, Craig 45%). Well balanced and
well shaped. It is also, in substance, a 45-second advertisement: the answer to
"what are the first steps" is "run your company on our Lucy visibility system"
and then the seven modules. The model's own reason says as much. Whether that is
right is the captain's call, not a defect - but it should not be counted as one
of the informational reels.

**18 - your-google-business-profile** (81.7s, Craig 35%). Solid, and it ends on
the word "customers" mid-thought rather than on a landing. Two near-repeats
inside it.

### Should not ship as selected

**3 - search-didnt-change-the-question-did** (37.7s after the detector fix).

Its span 301.2-341.3 contains **three takes of one sentence** and **two
complete calls to action**. Removing `MIN_TAKE_SECONDS` cuts three of the
repeats, and it still says "search didn't change, the question changed, whoever
AI understands best gets the answer" twice - at 306.8 and again at 328.6,
eighteen seconds apart with Craig's turn between. And it plays Craig's closer at
321.6 mid-reel, then Akshita's whole closer at 333.8-341.3 at the end.

**These are defects in the moment's span, not in any detector.** No cut rule
should silently remove a line eighteen seconds from its twin with another
speaker in between; that is an editorial call. The span needs re-drawing, or the
reel needs dropping.

The selector was not uninformed. Reel 3's proposal carries
`has_duplicate_take: true`, two similarity-1.0 findings, and a
`transcript_preview` showing the sentence four times. Its reason reads:

> "The tightest thing said in the episode and it is already whole"

## The shape of the answer

Of nineteen: seven read as reels somebody watches to the end, eleven have a
named, specific weakness, and one should not ship as selected. The commonest
weakness is the opening - four reels open on throat-clearing, a stumble, or a
back-reference to something outside the reel - and the second commonest is the
ending, where seven share one sentence.

Both of those are pipeline defects with fixes, not taste disagreements: the
handoff already states the hook rule and nothing measured whether it was
obeyed, and the handoff explicitly told the model closers were interchangeable
and said nothing about the reels being watched together.

## Reproducing this

    python3 -m library.tools.replay_bench capture <project> --id reels
    python3 -m library.tools.replay_bench compare reels select_reels \
        --rev-a <before> --rev-b <after>

Per-reel measurement uses `reel_build.reel_ranges` against the plan and the
timeline transcript. Nothing here needs Resolve, and nothing here writes to a
project.
