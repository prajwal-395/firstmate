## Best pick

**`keyword-stuffing-is-hurting-your-ai-visibility`, 127.86 - 192.39, 64.5 seconds. No borrowed CTA.**

Akshita opens on a dated audit of a real client whose SEO team stuffed every H1 tag with keywords. Google
loved it and was going to help them; AI read the same page as thin content, flagged it, and they stopped
appearing in AI answers at all. She states the law that follows - the thing you were doing to help your
Google rankings is actively hurting your AI recommendations on the spot. Craig names the tactic from the
outside ("late 90s early 2000s in marketing they call it keyword stuffing") and says the two systems are
working against each other. Akshita takes one step past that: they work against each other only if you do
not know how to work them together, and that is where the knowledge comes in. Craig closes on the offer.

Craig holds 24.3 of the 63.6 spoken seconds, 36%. Two speakers, four handoffs, one subject.

Two things put it first. It is **whole in one window** - the CTA is at 179.83-192.39, inside the body, so
nothing is placed after it and the reel plays as one continuous piece of the cut. And it **names a specific
artefact**: not "align your sources" but "they stuffed the H1 tags". Everything else in my top five borrows
its ending or generalises.

## The honest bar check on #1

1. **Does it open on something that is NOT throat-clearing?** Yes. First line: *"So we ran an audit last week
   on a client where we saw an SEO team stuff all the H1 tags with keywords."* A dated, concrete finding
   about a real client, complete inside four seconds. I deliberately did not start at Craig's question
   127.31 seconds earlier - *"give me an example of that difference"* is a better hook shape but "that
   difference" points at something the viewer has not heard, and coherence outranks shape.
2. **One idea developing, or one idea repeated?** One idea developing. The audit finding, then the mechanism
   (AI reads it as thin content), then the general law, then Craig naming it from outside, then Akshita's
   qualification that the two can be reconciled, then the invitation. Each step is new.
3. **Could a stranger follow it start to finish?** Yes. Nothing outside the window is required. "H1 tags",
   "thin content" and "keyword stuffing" are all explained by their use. The one inward reference - Craig's
   "as you said they are working against each other" - points at a line 20 seconds earlier inside the reel.
4. **Length band?** Yes. 64.5 seconds, against guidance of 45 to 90 and an average around a minute.
5. **Does it close on a CTA, and is it its own?** Yes and yes. Craig, 179.83-192.39: *"...so if you want to
   see how you're ranking how ai sees you go to our website the links in the bio."* Its own, in the window,
   contiguous with the body. Nothing borrowed.

## What is wrong with #1

The worst thing about it is **Akshita's 17.7-second qualification at 161.73-179.46**, which arrives directly
before the CTA and softens the reel's own claim. Having just said the two systems fight each other, she says
they only fight if you do not know how to make them work together. The sharpest possible version of this
material ends at 160.71 on Craig's line and runs 34.8 seconds.

I traded that sharpness for two things: a window that closes on its own spoken CTA instead of a grafted one,
and a set-up for the offer that the hard version does not have. A 35-second reel that ends on "they are
working against each other now" and then jump-cuts to an unrelated closer is a punchier 20 seconds and a
worse 45.

Second defect, smaller: an unbound stretch of Craig's speech (160.75-179.79) sits across Akshita's
qualification. If that is genuine murmur rather than the transcriber bridging a silent gap, there is a voice
under her there. My boundaries are both clear of it; I cannot tell from the data which it is.

## The honest global answer

**Yes - one.** `keyword-stuffing-is-hurting-your-ai-visibility` is genuinely good, not merely the best
available. The test I applied is whether it would work posted by an account I had never heard of, and it
does: it opens on a specific thing that happened to a specific client last week, it contains a reversal the
target viewer does not expect (the optimisation you paid for is the thing removing you), the reversal is
mechanically explained rather than asserted, both people in the room contribute to it, and it ends on an
invitation that grew out of the argument instead of being stapled to it. It is whole. Its ceiling is that
the delivery is conversational and unhurried and there is no number in it - it will not outperform its
category, but it is a real short rather than an excerpt.

Two others come close and fall short in ways worth naming, because both failures are informative.
`number-one-on-google-and-invisible-to-ai` has the strongest sentence in the episode and cannot end itself.
`your-competitor-is-ranking-nine-times-more` has the only figure with emotional weight and opens on 14
seconds of Craig winding up to the question.

Everything below about rank 10 is competent explainer content, and the obstacle there is the **footage**.
This is a re-recorded studio conversation in which six ideas circle - consistency across sources, AI reads
more than your website, niche beats generic, Google ranks pages and AI wants comprehension, hallucination is
an information problem, small beats big. Almost every exchange is a fresh angle on one of those six, which
is why my list has three reels about source consistency and two about the 20% figure. Volume was available;
variety was not recorded.

Two smaller obstacles, named precisely so they are not mistaken for the main one:

- **The footage, again, in a second way.** The retakes are the biggest single cost in this selection. Six of
  my thirty-one exist only because I could draw a window clear of a take the build will not remove, and one
  genuinely strong reel - the small-business/3D-nail-art exchange at 1186.94, whose direct address to a salon
  or gym owner is the best hook in the episode - could not be cut at all, because the build removes
  1219.06-1227.05 and strands the line after it, and every window that avoids the removal loses Craig.
- **The data I was given**, in one respect only. I could not tell whether 1200.59-1212.52 and 1643.68-1667.2
  are real crosstalk or one speaker's audio split across both diarization labels. That single fact decides
  whether the reel above is proposable. Nothing else I needed was missing.

Not the brief. The brief is unusually clear about what a reel is, what a CTA is for, and that overlap is
judged on meaning rather than seconds, and I never had to guess at any of it.
